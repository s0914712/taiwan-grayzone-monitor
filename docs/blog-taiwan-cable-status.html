🕸️ 蜘蛛網中的弱點

從空中俯瞰台灣的海底電纜網絡，它看起來像一張蜘蛛網——但不是一張平衡、均勻的網，而是一張有著明顯弱點的網。主要纜線從台灣本島的北部、東部和南部向外輻射，連接日本、菲律賓、關島和夏威夷，最終跨越太平洋延伸至美國西岸。

想像一個城市的自來水系統。假設這個城市有14條主幹管線從外部引水，但其中有幾條管線必須先經過一條非常狹窄的峽谷——一旦那個峽谷堵塞，不管有多少條管線，水都流不進來。台灣的海底電纜面對的，正是類似的「峽谷困境」：大量電纜必須通過台灣海峽這個相對狹窄的水域，形成了一個天然的戰略弱點。

更令人擔憂的是台灣離島的情況。馬祖、金門、澎湖這些距離台灣本島數十至數百公里的島嶼，它們與台灣的海底電纜連接更為脆弱——電纜條數少、替代路由有限，一旦斷纜，往往只能依賴頻寬極低的微波備援系統苦撐。2023年馬祖斷纜事件，是這一脆弱性最真實的預演。

數字的反差：台灣占全球海洋面積不到 0.13%，卻承受了全球約 10% 的海底電纜斷纜事件。這個比例，不可能僅僅是地理位置的偶然——它是台灣地緣戰略重要性與灰色地帶壓力的直接反映。

🕸️ Weak Points in the Spider Web

Viewed from above, Taiwan's submarine cable network looks like a spider's web — but not a balanced, even web. It's one with clear structural weak points. The main cables radiate outward from northern, eastern, and southern Taiwan, connecting to Japan, the Philippines, Guam, and Hawaii, eventually crossing the Pacific to the US West Coast.

Imagine a city's water supply system. Suppose this city has 14 main pipes drawing water from outside, but several of those pipes must first pass through a very narrow gorge — once that gorge is blocked, no matter how many pipes there are, water can't get in. Taiwan's submarine cables face a similar "gorge dilemma": many cables must traverse the relatively narrow Taiwan Strait, creating a natural strategic vulnerability.

Even more concerning is the situation of Taiwan's outlying islands. Matsu, Kinmen, and Penghu — islands located tens to hundreds of kilometers from mainland Taiwan — have far more fragile cable connections: fewer cables, limited alternative routes. When a cable breaks, they often have no choice but to rely on extremely low-bandwidth microwave backup systems. The 2023 Matsu cable cut was the most realistic rehearsal of this vulnerability.

A stark disparity: Taiwan accounts for less than 0.13% of global ocean area, yet absorbs approximately 10% of global submarine cable break incidents. This ratio cannot be mere geographical coincidence — it is a direct reflection of Taiwan's geostrategic importance and the gray zone pressures it faces.

🔌 台灣的14條國際電纜

台灣目前擁有14條活躍的國際海底電纜，連接範圍涵蓋美國、日本、菲律賓、新加坡、香港與東南亞各國。這個數字看似不少，但必須放在正確的脈絡下理解。

在容量面，這14條電纜中，部分使用年限較長、傳輸容量相對有限。台灣的網際網路骨幹在尖峰時期已接近飽和，若任何一條主要電纜斷裂，其流量必須被分攤至其餘電纜，有時會造成明顯的服務降速甚至中斷。

在路由多元性方面，這些電纜並非均勻地從各個方向連接台灣。台灣東北方向（前往日本、美國）集中了最多的國際頻寬，而台灣海峽方向的路由相對較少。當台灣海峽成為潛在衝突熱點時，這種路由集中恰恰形成了明顯的單點脆弱性。

容量並非多元性：擁有14條電纜並不等於擁有14條獨立路由。若多條電纜在台灣海峽的同一路段受損（如地震或密集拖錨），即使電纜總數龐大，仍可能造成通訊癱瘓。

🔌 Taiwan's 14 International Cables

Taiwan currently has 14 active international submarine cables, connecting to the United States, Japan, the Philippines, Singapore, Hong Kong, and Southeast Asia. This number sounds substantial, but it must be understood in context.

In terms of capacity, some of these cables are older with relatively limited bandwidth. Taiwan's internet backbone is already near saturation during peak hours; if any major cable breaks, its traffic must be redistributed among the remaining cables, sometimes causing noticeable slowdowns or even outages.

In terms of route diversity, these cables do not connect to Taiwan evenly from all directions. The northeast direction (toward Japan and the US) concentrates the most international bandwidth, while routes through the Taiwan Strait are fewer. As the Taiwan Strait becomes a potential conflict flashpoint, this route concentration creates a clear single-point vulnerability.

Some important international cables include:

Cable NameConnects ToNotes
APCN-2Japan, Korea, Hong Kong, SingaporeAsia-Pacific core trunk

Capacity is not diversity: Having 14 cables does not mean having 14 independent routes. If multiple cables are damaged in the same segment of the Taiwan Strait — by an earthquake or concentrated anchor dragging — even a large total number of cables can result in communications paralysis.

🏝️ 離島的脆弱性

如果說台灣本島的電纜網絡是一個「相對脆弱的蜘蛛網」，那台灣的離島就是被繩子懸掛在網上的幾滴水滴——只要那根繩子斷了，就什麼都沒有了。

馬祖列島 MATSU ISLANDS 最高風險 CRITICAL

人口約 1.3 萬。原有 2 條海底電纜連接台灣本島（馬祖台灣海底電纜 1 號與 2 號）。2023 年 2 月 8 日第 1 條斷，2 月 23 日第 2 條也斷。兩條全斷後，居民依賴微波備援通訊（頻寬約 10-50 Mbps，遠低於正常寬頻的 1 Gbps+）長達 52 天，直至修復完成。備援系統僅能支援政府與緊急通訊，一般民用網路幾乎癱瘓。

金門縣 KINMEN COUNTY 高風險 HIGH

人口約 14 萬（含外島）。僅有 1 條主要海底電纜連接台灣本島。雖有備援微波通訊，但一旦主纜斷裂，金門的數位能力將大幅受限。金門距廈門僅 2 公里，地理位置極為敏感，斷纜衝擊在戰略上不亞於馬祖。

澎湖縣 PENGHU COUNTY 中風險 MEDIUM

人口約 10 萬。有數條電纜連接台灣本島，冗餘度較馬祖、金門略高，但仍屬有限。2025 年 2 月，一艘懸掛多哥旗幟的貨輪（宏泰 58 號）涉嫌切斷連接澎湖的電纜，凸顯澎湖並非安全。

這三個案例共同說明了一個殘酷的現實：台灣的離島因地理孤立而天生脆弱。它們不只是旅遊勝地或歷史重地，它們是台灣防衛前線的前哨，也是最容易被以「斷纜」方式孤立、施壓的目標。

🏝️ Vulnerability of the Outlying Islands

If Taiwan's main island cable network is a "relatively vulnerable spider's web," then Taiwan's outlying islands are a few water droplets hanging from it by a single thread — once that thread breaks, there is nothing left.

MATSU ISLANDS CRITICAL RISK

Population approximately 13,000. Originally had 2 submarine cables connecting to mainland Taiwan (Matsu-Taiwan Submarine Cable 1 and 2). Cable 1 broke on February 8, 2023; Cable 2 broke on February 23. With both severed, residents relied on microwave backup communications (bandwidth roughly 10–50 Mbps, far below normal broadband's 1 Gbps+) for 52 days until repairs were complete. The backup system could only support government and emergency communications; ordinary civilian internet was nearly paralyzed.

KINMEN COUNTY HIGH RISK

Population approximately 140,000 (including outer islands). Has only 1 main submarine cable connecting to mainland Taiwan. Though microwave backup exists, once the main cable breaks, Kinmen's digital capability will be severely limited. Kinmen lies just 2 km from Xiamen, making its geopolitical sensitivity extreme; the strategic impact of a cable cut is no less than Matsu's.

PENGHU COUNTY MEDIUM RISK

Population approximately 100,000. Has several cables connecting to mainland Taiwan, with slightly more redundancy than Matsu or Kinmen — but still limited. In February 2025, a Togo-flagged cargo ship (Hong Tai 58) was suspected of cutting a cable connecting Penghu, demonstrating that Penghu is not safe either.

These three cases together illustrate a harsh reality: Taiwan's outlying islands are inherently vulnerable due to geographic isolation. They are not merely tourist destinations or historical sites — they are the front line of Taiwan's defense, and the targets most easily isolated and pressured through cable cutting.

🌊 電纜集中在台灣海峽

台灣海峽寬約180公里，平均水深不足100公尺——以海洋的標準來說，這是一片相當淺的水域，卻是台灣對外通訊的咽喉要道。大量海底電纜必須通過這片水域，形成了一個高度集中、易受攻擊的關鍵節點。

台灣海峽的兩個特點讓電纜特別脆弱：

水淺：大多數區域水深不足200公尺，意味著電纜雖有掩埋，但漁船拖錨、底拖網作業的深度完全足以觸及甚至切斷電纜。相較之下，太平洋深海（水深3,000-6,000公尺）的電纜幾乎不受這類威脅。

航運密集：台灣海峽是全球最繁忙的航運通道之一，每天有數百艘商船通過。密集的船隻也意味著密集的錨具作業風險，以及更高的「可否認性」——誰也說不清楚，那艘船到底是「意外走錨」還是「蓄意破壞」。

2025年1月，興順39號在台灣北部海域關閉AIS訊號，疑似拖錨切斷了Taiwan-Asia-US Cable。若非本站的持續監測留存了事前的異常行為軌跡，這次事件幾乎不可能被重建為「可疑行為」而非「意外事故」。

這正是監測的意義所在——不是等到電纜斷了才反應，而是在斷纜之前，就建立起可疑船舶行為的完整證據鏈。

🌊 Cable Routes Concentrated in the Taiwan Strait

The Taiwan Strait is approximately 180 kilometers wide, with an average depth of less than 100 meters — by ocean standards, quite a shallow body of water, yet it is the throat through which Taiwan's external communications must pass. Large numbers of submarine cables must traverse this area, creating a highly concentrated and vulnerable critical chokepoint.

Two characteristics of the Taiwan Strait make cables especially vulnerable:

Shallow water: Most of the area is less than 200 meters deep, meaning cables are buried but fishing vessel anchor dragging and bottom trawling reach depths more than sufficient to contact and sever them. By contrast, cables in the deep Pacific (3,000–6,000 meters depth) face virtually no such threat.

Dense traffic: The Taiwan Strait is one of the world's busiest shipping lanes, with hundreds of merchant ships passing through daily. Dense traffic also means dense anchoring risk, and higher "deniability" — no one can easily say whether that ship was an "accidental anchor drag" or "deliberate sabotage."

In January 2025, Xing Shun 39 disabled its AIS signal in waters north of Taiwan and allegedly dragged its anchor to sever the Taiwan-Asia-US Cable. Without this site's continuous monitoring having preserved the record of abnormal behavior beforehand, this incident would almost certainly have been reconstructed as an "accident" rather than "suspicious behavior."

This is exactly why monitoring matters — not reacting after the cable breaks, but building a complete chain of suspicious-vessel behavioral evidence before the break occurs.

📈 斷纜頻率急升

數字會說話，而台灣近年的斷纜統計數字，說的是一個令人不安的故事。

歷史上，台灣每年平均發生 7 至 9 次海底電纜斷纜事件。這個數字雖然已高於許多其他地區，但尚在「可管理」的範圍內——修復船能夠及時處理，斷纜影響通常在數日至數週內修復。

然而，近年的數字出現了令人憂慮的上升趨勢：

時期斷纜次數相對於歷史平均備注
歷史平均（2010-2022）7-9次/年基準含自然與意外因素
2023全年12次+50%含馬祖2次斷纜
2025年1-2月4次急升2個月即達平時半年水準

2025年1至2月的4次斷纜特別值得關注。這些事件並非均勻分布在年度中，而是集中在短短兩個月內爆發——包括興順39號切斷Taiwan-Asia-US Cable（2025年1月）、宏泰58號切斷澎湖附近電纜（2025年2月）。

統計上的「正常」斷纜率是每年7至9次，折算每月約0.6至0.75次。但2025年1至2月的速率是每月2次——幾乎是正常速率的3倍。這樣的集中程度，已讓許多安全專家認為，單純的「意外」解釋已難以成立。

台灣占0.13%的海洋面積，卻承受10%的全球斷纜事件：這個比例本身就說明了問題的嚴重性。台灣不只是海底電纜密集的地區，它還是灰色地帶壓力最集中的地區之一。斷纜頻率的急升，是這種壓力在數據上最直接的顯現。

📈 Soaring Cable Break Frequency

Numbers speak, and Taiwan's recent submarine cable break statistics tell a disturbing story.

Historically, Taiwan averages 7 to 9 submarine cable breaks per year. While already higher than many other regions, this was within "manageable" range — repair ships could respond in time, and most breaks were fixed within days to weeks.

However, recent years show a troubling upward trend:

PeriodBreak Countvs. Historical AverageNotes
Historical average (2010–2022)7–9 / yearBaselineIncludes natural and accidental factors
Full year 202312+50%Includes the 2 Matsu breaks
Jan–Feb 20254Spike2 months reached half a year's normal level

The 4 breaks in January–February 2025 deserve special attention. These incidents didn't spread evenly across a year — they concentrated within just two months, including Xing Shun 39 cutting the Taiwan-Asia-US Cable (January 2025) and Hong Tai 58 cutting a cable near Penghu (February 2025).

The statistically "normal" break rate is 7–9 per year, or roughly 0.6–0.75 per month. But January–February 2025's rate was 2 per month — nearly triple the normal rate. This concentration has led many security experts to conclude that a pure "accident" explanation is no longer tenable.

Taiwan covers 0.13% of ocean area yet absorbs 10% of global cable breaks: This ratio itself states the problem's severity. Taiwan is not only a region dense with submarine cables; it is also one of the most concentrated zones of gray zone pressure. The surge in break frequency is the most direct data expression of that pressure.

🛡️ 現有保護措施

面對上述威脅，台灣政府並非沒有採取行動，但現有措施仍有明顯不足之處。

海巡署巡防：海巡署艦艇定期巡邏台灣海峽與離島附近海域，若發現可疑船隻可進行攔查。然而，受限於艦艇數量與廣大的巡邏海域，實際的覆蓋率有限，無法24小時全天候監控所有電纜路線。

海軍協同：在特定情境下，海軍艦艇會協助保護重要電纜路線附近的水域，形成更強的嚇阻存在。但海軍的主要任務仍是整體海防，而非專門的電纜保護。

NCC監控：國家通訊傳播委員會（NCC）持續監測海底電纜的通訊狀況，若偵測到訊號異常能迅速通報。NCC也負責協調斷纜後的修復作業，調派修復船（通常需要從海外租借或調配）前往作業。

法規更新：2023年，台灣修訂電信管理法第72條，加重故意破壞電信基礎設施的罰則，並擴大海巡的執法授權。但如下一篇文章將詳述，這些規範在公海上的執法力仍十分有限。

修復船：台灣目前依賴與電纜業者的協議，調派專業修復船進行海底電纜維修作業。修復一次斷纜，往往需要數日至數週不等，取決於斷點深度、天候條件與船隻排程。

核心困境：所有現有措施都是「事後反應」——等到電纜已斷，才啟動修復與追責程序。在灰色地帶攻擊的邏輯下，攻擊者正是利用這個反應時間差，完成破壞後迅速離開，讓「犯罪現場」難以重建。

🛡️ Current Protection Measures

Facing these threats, Taiwan's government has not been idle — but existing measures have clear shortfalls.

Coast Guard patrols: Coast Guard vessels regularly patrol the Taiwan Strait and waters near outlying islands, able to intercept suspicious ships when detected. However, limited by fleet size and vast patrol areas, real coverage is constrained — 24/7 monitoring of all cable routes is not possible.

Navy coordination: In specific circumstances, Navy vessels assist in protecting waters near critical cable routes, providing stronger deterrence. But the Navy's primary mission remains overall maritime defense, not dedicated cable protection.

NCC monitoring: The National Communications Commission (NCC) continuously monitors cable communications; signal anomalies trigger rapid alerts. The NCC also coordinates post-break repair operations, dispatching repair ships (usually leased from abroad or repositioned) to the site.

Regulatory updates: In 2023, Taiwan amended Telecommunications Management Act Article 72, increasing penalties for deliberate damage to telecom infrastructure and expanding Coast Guard enforcement authority. But as the next article details, enforcement power on the high seas remains very limited.

Repair ships: Taiwan relies on agreements with cable operators to dispatch specialized repair vessels. Fixing a cable break typically takes days to weeks, depending on the depth of the break, weather conditions, and vessel scheduling.

The core dilemma: All existing measures are "reactive" — waiting until the cable is already broken before initiating repair and accountability procedures. In the gray zone attack logic, attackers exploit exactly this response lag: complete the sabotage, then quickly depart before the "crime scene" can be reconstructed.

🔭 未來展望

台灣在電纜韌性方面需要同時在多個層面推進：

硬體層面：為馬祖、金門增建電纜冗餘，優先評估在東海或南海方向增設電纜備援路由，降低對台灣海峽單一通道的依賴。此類工程需要數年規劃與龐大資金，但若不提前啟動，等到危機爆發時將為時已晚。

監測層面：建立整合AIS、SAR衛星影像與海底聲學感測器的全天候監測系統，在斷纜發生前就識別高風險船隻行為。本站的監測方法（詳見本系列第6篇）正是這個方向的實踐嘗試。

法律層面：積極透過友好國家在UNCLOS框架內尋求更強的執法機制，並推動旗幟國追責，讓懸掛坦尚尼亞、多哥等旗幟的可疑船隻不再能輕易逍遙法外。（詳見本系列第5篇執法框架文章）

外交層面：加強與美、日、澳等友邦的海底電纜安全合作，尋求軍事情報共享與海域監控協作，以彌補台灣單方面監測能力的不足。

台灣的海底電纜現況，是一個脆弱而正在被系統性挑戰的基礎設施體系。但認識脆弱性，是強化韌性的第一步。下一篇文章，我們將深入探討海底電纜所面臨的具體威脅類型——從鯊魚咬纜到影子船隊。

🔭 Looking Ahead

Taiwan needs to advance cable resilience on multiple fronts simultaneously:

Hardware: Build cable redundancy for Matsu and Kinmen; prioritize assessing new backup cable routes via the East or South China Sea to reduce dependence on the Taiwan Strait single corridor. Such engineering requires years of planning and substantial funding — but without starting early, it will be too late when crisis strikes.

Monitoring: Establish a 24/7 monitoring system integrating AIS, SAR satellite imagery, and underwater acoustic sensors that can identify high-risk vessel behavior before a cable breaks. This site's monitoring methodology (detailed in Article 6 of this series) is a practical attempt in that direction.

Legal: Actively seek stronger enforcement mechanisms within the UNCLOS framework through friendly states, and push for flag-state accountability so suspicious vessels flying Tanzanian, Togolese, or similar flags can no longer escape with impunity. (See Article 5 on the enforcement framework.)

Diplomacy: Strengthen submarine cable security cooperation with allies such as the US, Japan, and Australia, seeking military intelligence sharing and maritime surveillance coordination to compensate for Taiwan's unilateral monitoring limitations.
