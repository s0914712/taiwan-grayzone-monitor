The Enforcement Dilemma: Why Can't We Stop Shadow Vessels from Cutting Cables?

從國際法漏洞到台灣的法律工具與國際合作框架

From international law gaps to Taiwan's legal tools and cooperation frameworks

🚨 那艘消失的船

2025 年 1 月某個深夜，海巡署快速應變小組接獲通報，一艘懸掛坦尚尼亞旗幟的貨輪正在台灣北部海域的海底電纜路線附近異常徘徊。等到巡防艦艇趕抵現場，電纜已經斷了——而肇事船隻正高速駛向釜山港。

這就是興順 39（Xing Shun 39）事件的縮影，也是台灣執法困境最赤裸的寫照。當巡防艦終於追上這艘船時，船長一臉無辜：AIS？「設備故障了。」為什麼在電纜上方繞圈？「等待天氣好轉。」電纜怎麼斷的？「我們什麼都沒看到。」

更棘手的是，這艘船懸掛的是坦尚尼亞國旗。根據國際法，台灣（或任何沿海國）想要對一艘在其領海之外的外國籍船舶行使刑事管轄權，幾乎是不可能的任務。船還沒進港，就已經駛出了我國可以執法的範圍；即使攔下，要證明「故意」破壞、要取得船東配合、要讓船籍國願意協助調查——每一步都是死胡同。

於是，這艘船就這樣「消失」了。不是物理上的消失，而是從法律的視野中消失。它繼續航行、繼續做生意，彷彿什麼都沒發生。這不是執法人員不夠努力，而是整個國際法體系從根本上沒有為這種「灰色地帶攻擊」設計出有效的工具。

要理解為什麼，我們必須深入國際海洋法的核心結構。

🚨 The Ship That Got Away

Late one night in January 2025, the Coast Guard Administration's rapid-response team received a report that a cargo ship flying the Tanzanian flag was loitering abnormally near a submarine cable route in waters off northern Taiwan. By the time patrol vessels reached the scene, the cable was already severed — and the ship was speeding toward the port of Busan.

This is the essence of the Xing Shun 39 incident, and the starkest portrait of Taiwan's enforcement dilemma. When the patrol vessel finally caught up with the ship, the captain looked innocent: AIS? "Equipment failure." Why circling above the cable? "Waiting for better weather." How did the cable break? "We saw nothing."

Worse still, the ship flew the flag of Tanzania. Under international law, for Taiwan (or any coastal state) to exercise criminal jurisdiction over a foreign-flagged vessel beyond its territorial sea is nearly impossible. Before the ship even reached port, it had already sailed beyond the range where Taiwan could enforce; and even if stopped, proving "intent" to damage, securing the owner's cooperation, and getting the flag state to assist an investigation — every step is a dead end.

And so the ship simply "vanished." Not physically, but from the field of view of the law. It kept sailing, kept doing business, as if nothing had happened. This is not because the enforcers lacked effort, but because the entire international legal system was fundamentally never designed with effective tools for this kind of "gray zone attack."

To understand why, we must dive into the core structure of the international law of the sea.

⚖️ 國際法的結構性困境

《聯合國海洋法公約》（UNCLOS）是規範全球海洋秩序的根本大法，但正是它的幾項核心原則，反而成了影子船隊得以逍遙法外的「保護傘」。

UNCLOS 第 92 條 / 第 94 條：旗國專屬管轄＝執法真空
第 92 條確立了「旗國專屬管轄」原則——在公海上，船舶原則上只受其船籍國（旗國）的管轄。第 94 條則要求旗國對其船舶行使有效管轄與管制。問題在於：當旗國是一個監管寬鬆、毫無意願追究的「旗幟便利國」時，這套「專屬管轄」就變成了「沒有人管轄」的執法真空。
UNCLOS 第 113 條：刑事化義務，卻無執法機制
第 113 條要求各締約國立法，將「故意或因重大過失破壞公海海底電纜」的行為定為可處罰之罪。聽起來很好——但這只是「立法義務」，公約本身並未賦予沿海國在公海上攔截、登臨、逮捕外國籍肇事船的執法權力。換言之，有罪名，卻沒有抓人的手。
1884 年《保護海底電纜公約》第 8 條第 2 項：補充管轄權
這部更古老的公約其實提供了一個出路——第 8 條第 2 項允許締約國在一定條件下，對破壞電纜的他國船舶行使「補充管轄權」。然而致命的是：該公約僅有 36 個簽署國，而台灣與中華人民共和國都不是締約方。這個本可派上用場的法律工具，對台灣而言形同虛設。
「真正聯繫」原則（第 91 條）與旗幟便利國的濫用
第 91 條規定，船舶與其船籍國之間應存在「真正聯繫」（genuine link）。理論上，這應防止船舶隨意掛旗逃避監管。但實務上，這項原則缺乏強制力，旗幟便利國照樣大量註冊與其毫無實質關聯的外國船舶——興順 39 的坦尚尼亞旗、宏泰 58 的多哥旗，正是對「真正聯繫」原則的公然嘲諷。

綜合來看，國際法為影子船隊鋪設了一條近乎完美的脫罪路徑：掛上旗幟便利國的旗、在公海上作案、藏身於旗國專屬管轄之後。沿海國縱有滿腔義憤，也只能望船興嘆。這就是台灣執法困境的法理根源。

⚖️ The Structural Trap of International Law

The United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS) is the fundamental charter governing global maritime order, but it is precisely several of its core principles that have become the "umbrella" allowing shadow fleets to escape justice.

UNCLOS Art. 92 / 94: Flag-state exclusive jurisdiction = enforcement vacuum
Article 92 establishes the principle of "flag-state exclusive jurisdiction" — on the high seas, a ship is in principle subject only to the jurisdiction of its flag state. Article 94 requires the flag state to effectively exercise jurisdiction and control over its ships. The problem: when the flag state is a lax, unwilling "flag of convenience," this "exclusive jurisdiction" becomes an enforcement vacuum in which no one has jurisdiction.
UNCLOS Art. 113: Obligation to criminalise, but no enforcement mechanism
Article 113 requires states parties to legislate so that "wilfully or through culpable negligence breaking a submarine cable on the high seas" is a punishable offence. Sounds good — but this is only an "obligation to legislate"; the Convention itself grants the coastal state no power to intercept, board or arrest a foreign-flagged offending ship on the high seas. In other words, there is a charge, but no hand to make the arrest.
1884 Convention for the Protection of Submarine Cables, Art. 8(2): Supplementary jurisdiction
This older convention actually offers a way out — Article 8(2) allows states parties, under certain conditions, to exercise "supplementary jurisdiction" over foreign ships that damage cables. Fatally, however: the convention has only 36 signatories, and neither Taiwan nor the People's Republic of China is a party. This otherwise useful legal tool is, for Taiwan, effectively nonexistent.
The "genuine link" doctrine (Art. 91) and how flags of convenience abuse it
Article 91 provides that there must be a "genuine link" between a ship and its flag state. In theory this should prevent ships from flag-hopping to evade oversight. In practice the principle lacks enforcement teeth, and flags of convenience register vast numbers of foreign ships with no real connection — Xing Shun 39's Tanzanian flag and Hong Tai 58's Togolese flag are open mockeries of the "genuine link" principle.

Taken together, international law paves a nearly perfect path to impunity for shadow fleets: fly a flag of convenience, act on the high seas, and hide behind flag-state exclusive jurisdiction. The coastal state, however indignant, can only watch the ship sail away. This is the legal root of Taiwan's enforcement dilemma.

🛡️ 台灣的法律工具

儘管國際法的天花板很低，台灣國內法仍提供了若干可用的武器。問題在於它們的「射程」——大多只及於領海之內。

《電信管理法》第 72 條（2023 年修正）
這是台灣最直接的法律工具。2023 年修法後明定，破壞海底電纜或其設備屬刑事犯罪，最高可處五年以下有期徒刑。此舉大幅提高了蓄意破壞的刑責，也呼應了 UNCLOS 第 113 條的刑事化義務。

《刑法》毀損相關規定：對於毀損他人或公共財物、妨害公眾通訊等行為，刑法亦有相應處罰條款，可與電信管理法併同適用，強化追訴的法律基礎。

《海岸巡防法》：賦予海巡署在我國領海、鄰接區與經濟海域執行登檢、查緝、驅離等任務的法律授權，是第一線執法的依據。

致命限制——管轄範圍：上述工具的共同短板是地理範圍。它們主要適用於台灣的領海（12 浬）與部分鄰接區，一旦肇事船舶位於公海或迅速駛離，這些法律便鞭長莫及。興順 39 之所以能「逃脫」，正是因為它在我國有效管轄範圍的邊緣作案，並在執法力量趕到前就駛入了公海與他國港口。法律寫得再嚴，抓不到人也是枉然。

🛡️ Taiwan's Legal Arsenal

Although the ceiling of international law is low, Taiwan's domestic law still provides several usable weapons. The catch is their "range" — most reach only within the territorial sea.

Telecommunications Management Act, Art. 72 (amended 2023)
This is Taiwan's most direct legal tool. The 2023 amendment expressly makes damaging submarine cables or their facilities a criminal offence, punishable by up to five years' imprisonment. This sharply raised the penalty for deliberate sabotage and answers the criminalisation obligation of UNCLOS Article 113.

Criminal Code damage provisions: For damaging others' or public property and interfering with public communications, the Criminal Code also provides corresponding penalties that can be applied alongside the Telecommunications Management Act, strengthening the legal basis for prosecution.

Coast Guard Administration Act: This grants the Coast Guard Administration legal authority to board, inspect, interdict and expel within Taiwan's territorial sea, contiguous zone and economic waters — the basis for front-line enforcement.

The fatal limit — jurisdictional reach: The common weakness of these tools is geographic scope. They apply mainly within Taiwan's territorial sea (12 nm) and parts of the contiguous zone; once an offending ship is on the high seas or quickly departs, these laws cannot reach it. Xing Shun 39 "got away" precisely because it acted at the edge of Taiwan's effective jurisdiction and sailed into the high seas and a foreign port before enforcement arrived. No matter how strict the law on paper, it is futile if no one can be caught.

🔧 現行執法措施

面對法律工具的先天限制，台灣轉而強化「事前監測」與「跨部會協同」，試圖在攻擊發生前就辨識並嚇阻可疑船舶。

96 艘黑名單船舶：自 2024 年 9 月起，台灣開始系統性追蹤一份包含 96 艘可疑船舶的黑名單。這些船舶因船籍可疑、曾有異常行為或與影子船隊有關聯而被列管，一旦進入台灣周邊海域便會被重點監控。

海軍與海巡的協同：海軍負責較遠海域的偵察與態勢掌握，海巡署則執行近海的登檢與驅離。兩者透過情資共享與聯合演練，縮短從「發現異常」到「現場處置」的反應時間。

跨部會協調平台：海底電纜安全橫跨多個部門權責，台灣已建立涵蓋海洋委員會、交通部、國防部、國家安全會議等單位的跨部會協調機制，整合監測、外交、國安與通訊主管機關的力量，避免各自為政。

研議中的電纜保護區立法：為強化法律基礎，台灣正研議劃設「海底電纜保護區」的專法，限制船舶在電纜路線附近的下錨與作業行為，並對違反者課以更明確的責任。一旦立法，將有助於把「異常徘徊」從難以舉證的灰色地帶，轉化為可明確認定的違法行為。

🔧 Current Enforcement Measures

Faced with the inherent limits of legal tools, Taiwan has turned to strengthening "advance monitoring" and "interagency coordination," seeking to identify and deter suspicious vessels before an attack occurs.

96 blacklisted vessels: Since September 2024, Taiwan has systematically tracked a blacklist of 96 suspicious vessels. These ships are flagged for suspicious registry, prior abnormal behaviour, or links to shadow fleets, and are closely monitored whenever they enter waters around Taiwan.

Navy–Coast Guard coordination: The Navy handles reconnaissance and situational awareness in more distant waters, while the Coast Guard conducts near-shore boarding and expulsion. Through intelligence sharing and joint drills, the two shorten the response time from "spotting an anomaly" to "on-scene action."

Interagency coordination platform: Cable security spans the remit of many departments, and Taiwan has established an interagency mechanism covering the Ocean Affairs Council, Ministry of Transportation and Communications, Ministry of National Defense, and National Security Council, integrating monitoring, diplomacy, national security and communications authorities to avoid fragmentation.

Proposed cable protection zone legislation: To strengthen the legal basis, Taiwan is studying dedicated legislation to designate "submarine cable protection zones," restricting anchoring and operations near cable routes and imposing clearer liability on violators. Once enacted, this would help turn "abnormal loitering" from a hard-to-prove gray zone into a clearly identifiable unlawful act.

🌐 國際合作框架

既然單一國家難以獨力應對跨國影子船隊，國際社會近年積極建構多邊合作框架。台灣雖因政治處境難以正式加入，仍可透過理念相近國家間接參與。

紐約聯合聲明（2024，17 國）：2024 年，由 17 個國家共同發表的紐約聯合聲明，呼籲各國重視海底電纜安全、加強保護與韌性合作，是國際社會集體表態的重要里程碑。
QUAD 電纜連結夥伴關係：美、日、印、澳四方安全對話（QUAD）推動的電纜連結與韌性合作倡議，著眼於確保印太地區海底電纜的安全與多元路由。
G7 影子船隊工作組：七大工業國（G7）成立的影子船隊工作組，專責協調對規避制裁、危害基礎設施之可疑船隊的追蹤、資訊共享與制裁措施。
IMO 決議 A.1192(33)（2023）：國際海事組織（IMO）通過的此項決議，呼籲會員國打擊規避法規、危害安全的違規航運行為，為港口國管制提供了多邊依據。
東京備忘錄（Tokyo MOU）港口國管制：作為亞太地區的港口國管制（PSC）機制，東京備忘錄讓港口國得以對停靠之外國船舶進行安全與合規檢查，是攔截問題船舶的重要實務工具。

台灣的間接參與：受限於國際地位，台灣難以正式簽署上述多數框架。但透過與美、日等理念相近夥伴的情資交換、技術合作與非正式協調，台灣仍能在實質上對接國際反影子船隊的努力——本監測站所產出的開源情報，正是台灣可貢獻給國際社會的重要資產之一。

🌐 International Cooperation Frameworks

Since no single state can counter transnational shadow fleets alone, the international community has recently built multilateral cooperation frameworks. Although Taiwan's political situation makes formal accession difficult, it can still participate indirectly through like-minded states.

New York Joint Statement (2024, 17 nations): In 2024, the New York Joint Statement issued by 17 nations called for states to prioritise submarine cable security and strengthen protection and resilience cooperation — a major milestone of collective international resolve.
QUAD Cable Connectivity Partnership: The cable connectivity and resilience initiative driven by the Quadrilateral Security Dialogue (US, Japan, India, Australia) focuses on ensuring the security and route diversity of Indo-Pacific submarine cables.
G7 Shadow Fleet Task Force: The shadow fleet task force established by the G7 coordinates tracking, information sharing and sanctions against suspicious fleets that evade sanctions and endanger infrastructure.
IMO Resolution A.1192(33) (2023): This resolution adopted by the International Maritime Organization calls on member states to combat unlawful shipping practices that evade regulation and endanger safety, providing a multilateral basis for port state control.
Tokyo MOU port state control: As the port state control (PSC) mechanism for the Asia-Pacific, the Tokyo MOU lets port states inspect calling foreign ships for safety and compliance — an important practical tool for intercepting problem vessels.

Taiwan's indirect participation: Constrained by its international status, Taiwan can hardly formally sign most of these frameworks. But through intelligence exchange, technical cooperation and informal coordination with like-minded partners such as the US and Japan, Taiwan can still substantively connect to international counter-shadow-fleet efforts — and the open-source intelligence produced by this monitor is one of the key assets Taiwan can contribute to the international community.

📋 政策建議

綜合前述困境與既有措施，以下提出四項可強化台灣海底電纜保護的政策方向：

1. 補充管轄權立法：參考 1884 年公約第 8 條第 2 項的精神，台灣可在國內法中明文建立對破壞電纜之外國籍船舶的「補充管轄權」基礎，擴大可追訴的法律空間，縮小「公海＝無人管」的真空。

2. 對旗國施加外交壓力：針對坦尚尼亞、多哥等被反覆濫用的旗幟便利國，台灣應聯合理念相近國家，透過外交與多邊機制要求其落實「真正聯繫」與旗國管制義務，提高權宜船籍的政治成本。

3. 建立影子船隊資料庫並與 ICPC 共享：將現有 96 艘黑名單擴充為結構化、可持續更新的影子船隊資料庫，並與國際電纜保護委員會（ICPC）等專業組織共享，形成跨國的可疑船舶情資網絡。

4. 自動化電纜警報系統：建立如本監測站般的自動化異常船舶偵測與電纜警報系統，整合 AIS、SAR 衛星與威脅評分，在可疑船舶接近電纜路線時即時示警，把被動的「事後追查」轉為主動的「事前嚇阻」。

執法困境並非無解，而是需要法律、外交、科技三線並進。台灣或許無法改變國際法的結構，但可以透過精準的監測與證據保全，讓每一次灰色地帶攻擊都付出更高的曝光與政治代價——這正是本網站存在的核心使命。

📋 Policy Recommendations

Combining the dilemmas and existing measures above, here are four policy directions to strengthen Taiwan's submarine cable protection:

1. Supplementary jurisdiction legislation: Drawing on the spirit of Article 8(2) of the 1884 Convention, Taiwan can expressly establish in domestic law a basis for "supplementary jurisdiction" over foreign ships that damage cables, expanding the prosecutable legal space and narrowing the "high seas = no one's jurisdiction" vacuum.

2. Diplomatic pressure on flag states: For repeatedly abused flags of convenience such as Tanzania and Togo, Taiwan should join with like-minded states to demand, through diplomatic and multilateral mechanisms, that they fulfil "genuine link" and flag-state control obligations — raising the political cost of flags of convenience.

3. Build a shadow-fleet database and share with the ICPC: Expand the existing 96-vessel blacklist into a structured, continuously updated shadow-fleet database, and share it with professional bodies such as the International Cable Protection Committee (ICPC) to form a transnational network of suspicious-vessel intelligence.

4. Automated cable-alert system: Build an automated abnormal-vessel detection and cable-alert system like this monitor, integrating AIS, SAR satellites and threat scoring to issue real-time warnings when suspicious vessels approach cable routes — turning passive "after-the-fact tracing" into active "advance deterrence."

The enforcement dilemma is not unsolvable; it requires advancing on three fronts at once — law, diplomacy and technology. Taiwan may not be able to change the structure of international law, but through precise monitoring and evidence preservation it can make every gray zone attack pay a higher cost in exposure and political fallout — which is precisely the core mission behind this website.
