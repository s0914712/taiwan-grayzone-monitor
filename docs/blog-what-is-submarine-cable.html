第一節：馬祖的那個凌晨

2023年2月8日凌晨，馬祖島上的1萬3千名居民突然發現手機沒有訊號。不是電池沒電，也不是基地台故障——他們的島嶼，就這樣被切斷了與台灣本島、與世界的連結。

「我那時候在澳門出差，想打電話回家，怎麼打都打不通。家人說島上的網路全斷了，連 LINE 都沒辦法用。」——一位馬祖居民事後回憶道。

那天凌晨，一艘中國漁船在馬祖列島附近海域作業，拖錨切斷了連接馬祖與台灣本島的第一條海底電纜。11天後，2月23日，第二條——也是馬祖僅剩的那條——也遭到同樣的命運。

兩條電纜，全斷了。

從那一刻起，馬祖1萬3千名居民陷入了一場持續52天的數位孤立。他們只剩下一條微波通訊備援鏈路，頻寬相當於撥接時代的2G。想看YouTube？等上數分鐘。想視訊開會？幾乎不可能。政府機關的線上作業停擺，學生無法上網課，商家的信用卡機失靈。

這不是電影情節。這是2023年2月，發生在距離台北只有211公里的馬祖列島上的真實事件。

而這，就是海底電纜的故事——以及為什麼你必須了解它。

Section 1: The Morning Matsu Went Dark

In the early hours of February 8, 2023, the 13,000 residents of the Matsu Islands suddenly found their phones had no signal. It wasn't a dead battery or a failed cell tower — their islands had been severed from mainland Taiwan and the rest of the world.

"I was on a business trip in Macau and kept trying to call home. I couldn't get through. My family said the internet on the island was completely cut — even LINE didn't work." — A Matsu resident, recalling the incident afterward.

In the early morning hours, a Chinese fishing vessel operating near the Matsu archipelago dragged its anchor and severed the first submarine cable connecting Matsu to mainland Taiwan. Eleven days later, on February 23, the second — and only remaining — cable suffered the same fate.

Both cables. Gone.

From that moment, 13,000 residents of Matsu fell into a digital isolation that lasted 52 days. They were left with a single microwave communications backup link — bandwidth equivalent to a dial-up 2G connection. Watching YouTube? Minutes of waiting. Video conferencing? Nearly impossible. Government offices ground to a halt, students couldn't attend online classes, merchants' card readers stopped working.

This was not a movie plot. This happened in February 2023, on the Matsu Islands, just 211 kilometers from Taipei.

This is the story of submarine cables — and why you need to understand them.

第二節：海底電纜是什麼？

「海底電纜」這個名稱可能讓人聯想到一條粗大的鐵管橫臥在海床上。實際上，現代海底電纜的直徑往往只有約25至50毫米——比花園水管粗不了多少——但它的內部構造，卻是人類工程學的精華所在。

從外到內：電纜的七層防護

最外層：聚乙烯護套 防水、防腐、防生物附著
鋼絲鎧裝 抵抗漁網、錨具、船體刮擦
鋁合金水障 防止海水滲入內層
高密度聚乙烯 電氣絕緣層
銅導體 傳輸直流電力供給海底中繼器
光纖束（核心） 實際傳輸數據的光導纖維，每條電纜通常含4至16對光纖

光纖是整條電纜的靈魂。資料以光脈衝的形式在光纖中以接近光速的速度傳播。一條現代電纜可以同時傳輸數十Tbps（兆位元每秒）的數據——相當於每秒傳輸數百萬部高畫質電影。

海底中繼器：每隔70至100公里的心臟

光訊號在傳輸過程中會逐漸衰減。因此，每隔70至100公里，電纜上就設置一個「海底中繼器」（submarine repeater）——一個密封的金屬筒，內部有放大器，利用電纜內的銅導體傳來的電力，將光訊號放大後繼續向前傳送。一條跨越太平洋的電纜，可能有超過200個這樣的中繼器。

如何鋪設？

鋪纜船（cable laying vessel）是這項工程的主角。船上搭載電纜盤，在航行過程中緩慢將電纜沉入海底。在淺水區（水深小於1,000公尺），電纜會被特殊的水刀或犁具埋入海床下1至3公尺，提供額外保護。在深海區，電纜則直接鋪在海底，依靠深度本身提供保護——大多數商業船舶無法在那樣的深度下作業。

技術重點：台灣海峽北部水深多在100公尺以內的淺水區，電纜雖有掩埋保護，但漁船的拖錨作業深度足以觸及電纜，這正是馬祖斷纜事件反覆發生的物理原因之一。

Section 2: What Is a Submarine Cable?

The name "submarine cable" might conjure an image of a thick iron pipe lying on the seabed. In reality, modern submarine cables are typically just 25 to 50 millimeters in diameter — not much thicker than a garden hose — but their internal structure represents a pinnacle of human engineering.

From Outside In: The Seven-Layer Structure

Outermost: Polyethylene jacket Waterproofing, corrosion and biofouling resistance
Steel wire armor Resists fishing gear, anchors, and hull abrasion
Aluminum alloy water barrier Prevents seawater infiltration to inner layers
High-density polyethylene Electrical insulation layer
Copper conductor Powers the undersea repeaters via DC current
Fiber optic bundle (core) The actual data conduit; typically 4–16 fiber pairs per cable

Optical fiber is the soul of the cable. Data travels as light pulses at near the speed of light. A modern cable can simultaneously carry dozens of Tbps (terabits per second) — equivalent to transmitting millions of HD films every second.

Submarine Repeaters: A Heart Every 70–100 km

Light signals weaken over distance. Every 70 to 100 kilometers, the cable includes a "submarine repeater" — a sealed metal cylinder containing amplifiers, powered by the copper conductor's DC current, that boosts the optical signal and sends it onward. A trans-Pacific cable may carry more than 200 such repeaters.

How Are They Laid?

Cable-laying vessels are the workhorses of this engineering feat. Carrying enormous cable reels, they slowly pay out cable as they sail. In shallow water (less than 1,000 meters depth), special water-jet or plow systems bury the cable 1–3 meters beneath the seabed for extra protection. In deep water, cables rest on the seafloor — the depth itself provides protection, as most commercial vessels cannot operate at such depths.

Key Technical Note: The northern Taiwan Strait is mostly shallow water (under 100 meters depth). While cables there are buried, fishing vessels' anchor dragging can reach deep enough to contact the cable — this is one of the physical reasons why Matsu cable cuts recur.

衛星通訊當然存在，但其頻寬與成本根本無法與海底電纜相提並論。Starlink 等低軌衛星系統近年有所突破，但截至目前，全球所有衛星加起來的頻寬仍不到海底電纜的1%。這就是為什麼馬祖在兩條電纜斷裂後，只能用類似於2G的衛星備援——那已是當時最好的替代方案了。

金融系統的脆弱性：每日流經海底電纜的10兆美元金融交易，包括外匯市場、股票市場、跨境支付等，完全依賴這些鋪設在海床的電纜的穩定運作。一旦關鍵電纜遭到切斷，不只是網路斷線——整個金融系統都可能陷入癱瘓。

誰擁有這些電纜？

全球大多數海底電纜由私人企業或電信聯盟所有。近年來，Google、Meta、Amazon 等科技巨頭開始大量投資自建海底電纜，以確保其雲端服務的數據傳輸穩定性。台灣的主要海底電纜業者包括中華電信、遠傳電信、台灣大哥大等，以及多個國際電信聯盟。

Satellite communications exist, of course, but their bandwidth and cost cannot compare to submarine cables. Low-earth orbit systems like Starlink have made progress in recent years, but all satellites combined still carry less than 1% of submarine cable bandwidth. This is why after both of Matsu's cables were severed, residents were left with 2G-equivalent satellite backup — the best alternative available at the time.

Financial System Vulnerability: The $10 trillion in daily financial transactions flowing through submarine cables — foreign exchange markets, stock markets, cross-border payments — depends entirely on the stable operation of cables laid on the ocean floor. If a critical cable is severed, it's not just an internet outage: entire financial systems can seize up.

Who Owns These Cables?

Most global submarine cables are owned by private companies or telecommunications consortia. In recent years, tech giants like Google, Meta, and Amazon have invested heavily in building their own dedicated cables to ensure stable data transmission for cloud services. Taiwan's major cable operators include Chunghwa Telecom, Far EasTone, and Taiwan Mobile, alongside multiple international consortia.

第四節：台灣的特殊脆弱性

台灣擁有14條國際海底電纜與10條國內海底電纜，表面上看起來相當充裕。但當你把這些電纜的路徑畫在地圖上，你會立刻看出一個令人不安的問題：它們高度集中在幾個關鍵節點上。

台灣本島的電纜大量集中在台灣海峽這條狹窄的水道上。台灣海峽最窄處只有約130公里，是全球商船密度最高的航道之一，同時也是中國漁船、軍事船隻、海警船日常出沒的區域。這種地理現實使得電纜保護工作面臨極大的挑戰。

馬祖的困境：世界上最脆弱的數位連結

馬祖的案例是台灣電纜脆弱性的極端縮影。只有2條電纜連接著這座島嶼的1萬3千名居民與外界。這2條電纜——馬祖至烏坵海底電纜與馬祖至馬尾的電纜——穿越的正是中國漁船作業最頻繁的海域。

比較視角：台灣14條國際電纜的登陸點集中在少數地點，若其中幾個節點同時遭到破壞，修復時間往往需要數週乃至數月。相比之下，美國本土擁有超過60條國際電纜，分散在東西兩岸大量登陸點，韌性遠高於台灣。

斷纜頻率急遽上升

台灣年均電纜斷裂次數為7至9次，這在全球算是偏高的比例，反映出台灣海峽高強度的船舶活動對電纜的磨損與破壞。然而，2023年突然跳升至12次，2025年僅1至2月就已發生4次，顯示斷纜事件已不再只是「意外」的統計常態，而是出現了某種系統性的加速趨勢。

Section 4: Taiwan's Unique Vulnerability

Taiwan has 14 international submarine cables and 10 domestic ones — which sounds reasonably robust. But when you plot those cables on a map, a troubling pattern immediately emerges: they are highly concentrated at a handful of critical nodes.

Geographic Concentration Risk

AreaInternational CablesBackup StatusRisk Level
Taiwan Main Island (W/N landings)14 (shared)Multi-cable redundancyMedium
Matsu Islands2Microwave backup (low bandwidth)Extreme
Kinmen Islands11 backupHigh
Penghu3Partial redundancyMedium

Taiwan's main island cables are heavily concentrated in the narrow Taiwan Strait corridor. The strait's narrowest point is only about 130 kilometers wide — one of the highest-density shipping lanes in the world, and a daily operational zone for Chinese fishing vessels, military ships, and coast guard vessels. This geographic reality creates enormous cable-protection challenges.

The Matsu Dilemma: The World's Most Vulnerable Digital Link

Matsu's case is the extreme embodiment of Taiwan's cable vulnerability. Only 2 cables connect the archipelago's 13,000 residents to the outside world. These 2 cables — the Matsu-Wuqiu and Matsu-Mawei cables — traverse precisely the waters most heavily fished by Chinese vessels.

Comparative Perspective: Taiwan's 14 international cable landings are concentrated at a few locations. If several nodes were simultaneously damaged, repair time could run weeks to months. By contrast, the continental US has over 60 international cables spread across numerous landing points on both coasts, far more resilient than Taiwan's configuration.

Cable Break Frequency Surging

Taiwan's annual average of 7–9 cable breaks is already high by global standards, reflecting the intensive vessel traffic and cable wear in the Taiwan Strait. But the 2023 jump to 12, followed by 4 breaks in just January–February 2025, suggests that incidents are no longer just statistical noise — a systematic acceleration has emerged.

第五節：從基礎設施到戰略武器

一條被切斷的海底電纜，表面上是「意外」，但它的效果，可以是戰略性的。

數位孤立：資訊戰的第一步

當一個地區的海底電纜被切斷，最直接的後果是網路中斷。但更深層的影響在於：切斷通訊，就是切斷訊息。一旦台灣離島的居民無法即時獲得外界訊息，謠言、恐慌、錯誤資訊就會填補空白。這正是現代信息戰的核心邏輯：在對方的認知環境中製造混亂。

經濟戰：打擊金融命脈

台灣是全球半導體供應鏈的核心，台積電等企業的晶片訂單、設計文件、生產調度，每天都需要透過海底電纜與全球合作夥伴同步。電纜一旦遭到大規模破壞，這些即時通訊連結就會中斷，不僅影響台灣的科技產業，也會波及全球供應鏈。

灰色地帶戰略的精髓：海底電纜破壞是「灰色地帶戰術」的典型應用——行動本身難以確認是蓄意還是意外，難以歸因，難以在國際法框架下追究責任。施行者可以在不引發正式武裝衝突的情況下，持續對對手造成經濟與資訊上的損害。

軍事準備的降維打擊

在台灣海峽衝突情境的沙盤推演中，海底電纜是最先被考慮切斷的目標之一。台灣的軍事指揮、情報傳輸、對外協調，均高度依賴穩定的通訊基礎設施。如果在衝突開始前或初期，關鍵電纜遭到系統性破壞，台灣的對外協調能力將大幅削弱。

斷纜的根源：41%是漁業活動

當然，並非所有斷纜事件都是蓄意破壞。根據國際電纜保護委員會（ICPC）的統計，全球電纜斷纜事件中：

原因佔比說明
漁業活動（拖網/拖錨）41%最常見原因，台灣海峽漁船密度極高
原因不明18%無法確認，部分可能涉及蓄意破壞
船錨拖刮16%商船錨具在淺水區觸及電纜
敵對第三方（蓄意破壞）9%有充分理由認為屬蓄意行為
自然因素16%地震、海底滑坡、腐蝕等

即使只是「正常」的41%漁業拖錨，在台灣海峽的政治脈絡下，也充滿爭議。尤其當中國漁船在關閉AIS訊號、進行難以監控的夜間作業時，「意外」與「蓄意」之間的界線，根本無從確認。

Section 5: From Infrastructure to Strategic Weapon

A severed submarine cable appears, on the surface, to be an "accident." But its effects can be entirely strategic.

Digital Isolation: The First Step of Information Warfare

When a region's submarine cables are cut, the most immediate consequence is an internet outage. But the deeper impact: severing communications means severing information. Once residents of Taiwan's outlying islands cannot receive real-time information from the outside world, rumors, panic, and misinformation rush in to fill the vacuum. This is the core logic of modern information warfare: create cognitive chaos in the adversary's information environment.

Economic Warfare: Striking the Financial Lifeline

Taiwan is the core of the global semiconductor supply chain. TSMC and other companies' chip orders, design files, and production schedules need to sync daily with global partners through submarine cables. Large-scale cable damage breaks those real-time communication links — harming not only Taiwan's tech industry but the entire global supply chain.

The Essence of Gray Zone Strategy: Submarine cable sabotage is a textbook gray zone tactic — the action itself is hard to confirm as deliberate vs. accidental, hard to attribute, and nearly impossible to prosecute under international law. The actor can inflict sustained economic and information damage on an adversary without triggering a formal armed conflict.

Degrading Military Preparedness

In Taiwan Strait conflict scenario planning, submarine cables are among the first targets considered for severing. Taiwan's military command, intelligence transmission, and external coordination all depend heavily on stable communications infrastructure. If key cables are systematically damaged before or in the early stages of conflict, Taiwan's ability to coordinate with external partners would be severely compromised.

The Root Causes: 41% from Fishing Activity

Of course, not all cable breaks are deliberate sabotage. According to the International Cable Protection Committee (ICPC), global cable break causes break down as follows:

CauseShareNotes
Fishing activity (trawl/anchor drag)41%Most common cause; Taiwan Strait has extremely high fishing vessel density
Unknown cause18%Unverifiable; some may involve deliberate sabotage

Even the "normal" 41% of fishing-related anchor drags carry controversy in the Taiwan Strait's political context. Especially when Chinese fishing vessels operate at night with AIS signals off, making monitoring nearly impossible — the line between "accident" and "deliberate act" simply cannot be established.

Data Sources: Taiwan Port Bureau AIS real-time data + Global Fishing Watch SAR satellite imagery + CSIS "Signals in the Swarm" methodology, combined into a comprehensive threat assessment.

Threat Scoring System

This site scores each vessel across multiple threat indicators:

  • Cable proximity (within 5km of cable): +2 pts
  • Loitering near cable (<8 kn speed, >3 hrs): +3 pts
  • Zigzag pattern (≥3 turns of ≥45°): +1 pt
  • Near 200m depth contour (shelf edge): +1 pt
  • AIS anomalies (going dark, spoofing, identity changes): up to +3 pts
  • UN sanctions vessel: +8 pts
  • AIS position spoofing (impossible physics): +4 pts

Risk levels: Critical ≥12 | High ≥8 | Medium ≥5 | Normal <5
